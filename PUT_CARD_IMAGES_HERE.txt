I’d recommend using Canva because it makes the sizing and exporting very simple.
Create a 325 × 325 design. In Canva, choose Create a design → Custom size → 325 px × 325 px. Make sure the units say px, not inches.
Keep every card square. Don’t make some 325×325 and others different sizes. Every definition and picture card should use the exact same dimensions.
Use a simple background. White or another very light background is safest. Avoid busy textures or backgrounds that compete with the information.
Leave some empty space around the edges. Keep important text and pictures roughly 20–25 pixels away from the edge. That prevents cards from looking cramped when they enlarge.
For definition cards, make the text BIG. Students are viewing these on Chromebooks. Use a clear font such as Arial, Aptos, Montserrat, or similar. Avoid decorative fonts. Try to use the largest font that allows the definition to fit comfortably.
Keep definitions short whenever possible. Instead of squeezing a paragraph onto the card, use concise wording. For example, this:
A structure that controls the activities of the cell and contains genetic material.
is much better than several sentences of explanation.
Center the text vertically and horizontally unless there is a reason not to. Definition cards look much cleaner when all of them follow the same layout.
For picture cards, make the object as large as practical. A microscope image, organelle, diagram, graph, etc. should occupy most of the card. Avoid tiny pictures floating in a lot of blank space.
Avoid putting the answer on the picture. If a card is supposed to match “mitochondrion,” don't have the word MITOCHONDRION printed on the picture unless identifying labels are part of the lesson.
Duplicate the first card instead of starting over. Once your first card looks good, use Duplicate page in Canva. Replace the text or image but leave the size, margins, font, and general layout alone. This gives the whole set a professional appearance.

325 × 325 PNG + large readable content + consistent layout + exact filenames.
